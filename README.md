# Counterexamples to Zoroa et al.'s strong-dominance conjecture for every number of draws above four

**Authors:** Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

We refute the strong-dominance conjecture of Zoroa, Lesigne, Fernández-Sáez, Zoroa and Casas (arXiv:1608.00155v1, Section 8, Conjecture, page 30) for every number of labels N≥4 and every number of independent draws n≥5, with strictly positive rational coupon probabilities. For n=5 and p=(48,48,2,2)/100 and q=(50,46,3,1)/100, one has p≺q, but the probability mass under p is larger at two distinct labels and smaller at three distinct labels. These two inequalities exclude every real single-crossing threshold. The obstruction persists along a rational one-parameter family on a full real interval. A Taylor estimate extends it to every number of draws n≥5, and adding sufficiently rare labels gives strictly positive counterexamples in every dimension N≥4. The cumulative distributions retain the usual stochastic order. On supports of at most three distinct counts, that order implies the conjectured conclusion.
